Deep Dive

What the data told us

Three data stories that made the solution inevitable.

01

Most users chose the fastest. Fast wasn't reliable enough.

Aadhaar Lite commanded 70% of all attempts — users self-selected the lowest friction option. But even the preferred method was failing 1 in 2 users. OCR, the fallback, was both the slowest and least successful.

The method users trusted most still failed nearly half the time.

02

Failures weren't random. 40% came from one fixable problem.

Name mismatches alone accounted for 40% of all PAN verification failures. The top 5 error types together made up over 99% of all failures. These weren't edge cases. They were the norm.

A design problem disguised as a data problem.

03

The QR idea died on a coverage problem, not a concept problem.

The Secure QR format with full demographic data and photo only became standard from 2019 onwards. Users with Aadhaar last downloaded before that had QR codes with incomplete data. For a base of 59M+ min-KYC users, the gap was too wide to ship on.

A great idea. The wrong moment in history.

~15M Users with pre-2017 Aadhaar, no secure QR
The Takeaway

"Data didn't just inform the solution. It eliminated everything else."

Final Solution

We moved the ask. Everything else followed.

KYC was interrupting users before they'd committed to anything. No team built. No contest joined. Just a government document form, at the worst possible moment.

Reg → Home → Contest listing → Gameplay → KYC + Location → Payment
Screen 1

Screen 2

Screen 3
Verify Govt. ID
+30% cash conversion

Moving KYC to post-team-creation, after the user had already invested time building their squad, improved top-of-funnel cash conversion by 30%. Users who'd built a team were far more motivated to complete verification than users who hadn't started yet. Intent was real. The ask finally matched it.

Explored & Dropped

Roads not taken.

Four ideas that shaped the solution by not making it.

DROPPED
qr_code_scanner
Dropped

Aadhaar QR Scan

Scan the QR, auto-fill the fields. Zero typing, zero uploads. But the Secure QR format with full demographic data only arrived in 2019, and a significant portion of our user base had older cards that wouldn't work. Coverage gap was too large to ship on.

Killed by Coverage gap

DROPPED
link
Dropped

Aadhaar + PAN, No OTP

Since PAN-Aadhaar linkage is government-mandated, two numbers the user already knows could uniquely verify identity: no document upload, no OTP dependency. Clean and low friction. But it couldn't derive a user photo, failing the full KYC requirement.

Killed by No photo derivation

DROPPED
document_scanner
Dropped

OCR with Inline Correction

Instead of OCR failing silently, surface what it read and let users fix it upfront, like Zomato does with address parsing. The concept was sound. The reality wasn't: Voter IDs and Driving Licences have completely different layouts across every Indian state. Too many variants, too little time.

Killed by India's document layout chaos

DROPPED
id_card
Dropped

Interactive Document Card UI

What if instead of a flat form, users tapped fields directly on a visual representation of their ID, spatial, intuitive, document-native? The idea was strong. But rendering a consistent card UI across every document type, on three platform variants (iOS, APK, Play Store), made it technically infeasible within the timeline.

Killed by Tech complexity + timeline

"The solution got sharper every time we killed an idea."

Navigating Uncertainty

Adapting to Rapid
Regulation Changes

The rules changed while we were building. Three times. Here's what we kept, what we scrapped, and what we rebuilt from scratch.

remove Initial

Withdrawals Only

We started with withdrawal flows only — helping users get money out. Deposits came later, which meant rebuilding the funnel halfway through.

gavel Pivot

Tamil Nadu Law Changes

Tamil Nadu banned real-money gaming mid-project. Two flows we'd already designed for that state had to be scrapped entirely.

account_balance_wallet Final

Deposit and Withdrawal Revamp

The final scope: a full deposit and withdrawal revamp, with KYC now gating both directions. Bigger than what we signed up for, but cleaner for users.

shield_lock
Regulatory Reality

"In regulated industries, design flexibility isn't nice-to-have-it's survival."

Building in components meant we could swap out the Tamil Nadu flow, rebuild the deposit gate, and absorb a scope change — without starting over each time.

Reflection

Earned lessons

visibility

Show and tell beats tell alone

The 'more steps = more friction' pushback only resolved when I mapped the flow visually - showing the click count staying the same.

architecture

Design work can be made obsolete by external factors

Two flows - the TN location design and the full PAN-at-KYC flow - didn't ship as intended due to RMG ban and regulatory changes.

insights

Track the 28% who still fail

55% → 72% real progress. But 28% still hit a wall. We didn't instrument follow-through on those users post-launch.
