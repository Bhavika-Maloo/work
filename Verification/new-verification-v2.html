Withdrawal

The old withdrawal flow

When users came to withdraw after a win, they hit a chain of verification walls they'd never been warned about — PAN, KYC, and bank names all had to match, discovered only at the moment of payout.

Symptom 01 — Withdrawal walls

Users reached withdrawal and discovered their PAN, KYC, and bank account names had to match. By then, the mismatch was already baked in. Nothing at deposit had warned them.

Symptom 02 — PAN entry failures

88% of PAN verification failures were data-entry mismatches. Users were re-typing names the system already had on file from prior KYC.

Symptom 03 — Re-verification fatigue

Already-verified users were being asked to verify again to satisfy the upcoming regulation. Each touchpoint behaved as if prior verification didn't exist.

Research

How we got there

Quantitative

Cohort distribution by PAN/Aadhaar status. PAN failure mode breakdown — which revealed that 88% of failures were UI-addressable, not technical. TAT and automation rates from the data team.

Qualitative

Moderated user research with the existing verification flow. One quote that shaped our approach: "AADHAR OTP misuse ho sakta hai (camera access karke)" — users feared misuse, not the data sharing itself.

Guerilla

N=19 in-person sessions on document priority. Result: Driving Licence preferred (most handy), then Voter ID, then PAN. Passport rarely usable.

Design Principle

The old system asked users to remember things across steps that took weeks to traverse.

The new system makes the system itself remember — and warn ahead, fill in behind.

Final Solution

Deposit

Step-by-step guidance

Reg → Home → Contest listing → Gameplay → KYC + Location → Payment
+30% cash conversion

Moving KYC to post-team-creation, after the user had already invested time building their squad, improved top-of-funnel cash conversion by 30%. Users who'd built a team were far more motivated to complete verification than users who hadn't started yet. Intent was real. The ask finally matched it.

UX Strategy & Product Design

Step-by-step guidance

Restructuring the journey so verification happens at the moment of highest user commitment.

clinical_notes Evolution Analysis

Option overload → to guided completion

Reducing cognitive load through progressive disclosure and smart ID path routing.

User Experience Strategy

Reduce choice, route on failure

Rather than presenting all options upfront, the flow surfaces the highest-SR path first and routes intelligently on failure - using backend success rates, not guesswork.

Explored & Dropped

Roads not taken.

Four ideas that shaped the solution by not making it.

qr_code_scanner
Dropped

Aadhaar QR Scan

Zero typing, zero uploads. Scan and auto-fill. Dropped because older Aadhaar cards lacked the Secure QR format, leaving too large a coverage gap to ship.

Killed by Coverage gap

link
Dropped

Aadhaar + PAN, No OTP

No uploads, no OTP. Two numbers users already know. Clean in theory, but it couldn't derive a photo, which failed the full KYC requirement.

Killed by No photo derivation

document_scanner
Dropped

OCR with Inline Correction

Show what OCR read, let users fix it. Sound concept, but Voter IDs and Driving Licences have wildly different layouts across states, too many variants to handle in time.

Killed by India's document layout chaos

id_card
Dropped

Interactive Document Card UI

Tap fields on a visual ID card, not a flat form. Intuitive, but rendering it consistently across all document types and three platform variants made it technically infeasible within the timeline.

Killed by Tech complexity + timeline

Reflection

Earned lessons

visibility

Show and tell beats tell alone

The 'more steps = more friction' pushback only resolved when I mapped the flow visually - showing the click count staying the same.

architecture

Design work can be made obsolete by external factors

Two flows - the TN location design and the full PAN-at-KYC flow - didn't ship as intended due to RMG ban and regulatory changes.

insights

Track the 28% who still fail

55% → 72% real progress. But 28% still hit a wall. We didn't instrument follow-through on those users post-launch.
