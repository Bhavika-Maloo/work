Product Context

Fantasy sports is supposed to be about glory, trash talk, and proving you're smarter than your friends. But before any of that happens, someone has to actually create the league. Classic Fantasy League Creation already allowed users to deeply customise leagues across team size, scoring, transfers, and updates. Over time, this flexibility turned league creation into a powerful but complex surface, primarily used by experienced creators.

The Two-Headed User Problem:
A Social Contract

Designing a league creation flow isn't like designing a solo checkout experience. It's more like drafting a legal contract between friends, but with more swearing and higher stakes. I realized early on that we weren't just designing for one person; we were designing a handshake between two very different mindsets. If either side felt the deal was "unfair" or too confusing, the whole thing would collapse.

Audit

Auditing the Chaos — The "Crime Scene"

Before we could build a better version, I had to spend some quality time with the original UI. It wasn't "bad" in a way that didn't work—it was bad in a way that made you feel like you were filling out an insurance claim.

Issue 01

No Sense of Progression

What's happening

Setup used static tabs for "Basic Info," "Team Rules," and "League Rules."

Why it's broken

Tabs work for settings, but fail for sequential creation flows requiring a start and end.

Impact

Users skipped critical steps or felt no sense of completion as they navigated blindly.

No Progress Feedback

Issue 02

Duration Mismatch

What's happening

Duration picker used technical match numbers like "Match 4-72" as primary labels.

Why it's broken

Users think in timeframes ("This Weekend") or season phases, not arbitrary match indices.

Impact

Forced mental math at the point of entry, creating immediate cognitive friction.

Mental Model Mismatch

Issue 03

Fragmented Rule System

What's happening

Game rules were scattered across multiple disjointed tabs without clear hierarchy.

Why it's broken

Context is lost when user-facing rules are separated from the league structure they define.

Impact

Information siloization led to user confusion and "tab-hopping" to verify logic.

Siloed Context

Issue 04

Opaque Gameplay Logic

What's happening

Toggles for boosters and C/VC (Captain/Vice-Captain) existed without inline explanations.

Why it's broken

Assumes expert knowledge in a flow meant to onboard potential new league creators.

Impact

New users defaulted to settings they didn't understand, leading to poor league balance.

Hidden Calculation

Issue 05

Review Step Failure

What's happening

The final confirmation was a technical "data dump" summary screen.

Why it's broken

It lacked visual storytelling or a "vibe check" of what the created league would feel like.

Impact

High abandonment rate at the final step due to lack of emotional payoff or reassurance.

Auction Design

Designing auctions is about choosing tradeoffs

The "Auction feel" must prioritize the visceral energy of a high-stakes arena while maintaining mechanical integrity.

Balance 01

Live vs Silent

We went with Live. We wanted the chaotic energy of real-time psychological warfare, not the "strategic precision" of a sealed envelope.

Balance 02

Open vs Hidden

We chose Open Competition. If you can't see your opponent's budget, the "excitement" turns into "frustration" real fast.

Balance 03

Excitement vs Speed

The clock is a weapon. We had to find the sweet spot where a timer builds narrative tension without making the draft last five hours.

Balance 04

Fairness vs Flexibility

We chose Strict Guardrails. In a high-stakes arena, "human deals" lead to "human arguments." We needed the system to be the unbiased referee.

Final Decisions

bolt

Live Bidding

Prioritizing real-time psychological warfare. Every bid is visible, every second counts.

groups

Total transparency. Managers see who is bidding and how much remains in their budget.

Auction League Creation

Building a New Mode on a Familiar System

Auction league creation was built from scratch to support bidding-based gameplay. The challenge wasn't just adding auction mechanics, it was introducing them without breaking how league creation already works.

Step 01

The "Don't Make Me Relearn" Rule

lightbulb

Insight: A new mode shouldn't force users to relearn how to create a league.

What changed

  • We kept the core sequence (Duration → Participants → Rules) exactly the same. Users who had created Classic leagues felt an immediate sense of "I know where I am."
  • • Retained the same step sequence and navigation
  • • Reused familiar interaction patterns
